How Much Topsoil Do You Need to Topdress a Lawn?

By Syed Mohsin Ali

Topdressing a lawn means spreading a thin layer of soil over existing grass to level low spots, smooth bumps, and give seed good contact with the soil. Ordering by guesswork wastes money: too little stops the job halfway, and too much leaves soil to store or move again. Measure the lawn, choose a depth, and work out the volume before you order.

Start with the Topsoil Calculator. Enter the length and width of the area, then the depth you plan to spread. For an existing lawn, keep each pass thin, about one to two inches, so the grass can grow through. The formula is simple: cubic yards equal length times width times depth in feet, divided by 27, because a cubic yard holds 27 cubic feet.

Take the calculator's own example: a bed 25 feet long and 10 feet wide, filled 3 inches deep. The area is 250 square feet. Three inches is 0.25 feet, so the volume is 62.5 cubic feet. Divide by 27 and you get 2.31 cubic yards. Add the usual 10% allowance for uneven ground and settling, and the order becomes 2.55 cubic yards, or 68.75 cubic feet.

Weight follows from a density of 2,100 pounds per cubic yard. Before the buffer, the soil weighs 4,861 pounds (2.43 tons); with the buffer, it weighs 5,347 pounds (2.67 tons). In bags, the buffered volume needs 92 of the 0.75 cubic foot bags, at $2.98 each, for $274.16. The 1.0 cubic foot bags need 69 bags, at $3.85 each, for $265.65.

Bulk is cheaper once the job grows: 2.55 cubic yards at $35 per yard is $89.25, plus $80 delivery, for $169.25, a saving of about $105 over bags. A 600 square foot lawn at the same depth needs 6.11 cubic yards, or 220 bags, so that is a bulk job.
